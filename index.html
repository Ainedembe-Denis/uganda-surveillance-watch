Framework Mitigation Matrix

Framework Layer Proactive Controls (Prevention) Reactive Controls (Response)
1. User Empowerment
  • Point-of-Sale Onboarding: Mandating device retailers provide baseline digital hygiene literacy.
  • Public & High-Risk Training: Specialized training for the general public and high-risk targets on spyware and surveillance risk and how to protect themselves.
  • Clear Reporting Mechanisms: A structured interaction flow (Suspicion → Isolation → Trusted Helpline → Professional Forensics) linking users to experts for safe triage and TFGBV support.
2. Organizational Governance
  • High-Target Auditing: Strict Zero-Trust MDM preventing corporate/personal data mixing.
  • Awareness Decentralization: Expanding mandates beyond corporate silos to the informal economy.
  • Spyware Playbooks: Formal incident response protocols for nation-state infections.
  • Digital Lender Accountability: Strict audits for platforms unauthorizedly scraping SMS/notifications.
3. Technical Infrastructure
  • State-Provisioned Tooling: Government procurement of detection tools distributed via public services.
  • Import Checks: Requirements for pre-installed anti-surveillance baselines on imported devices.
  • Hardware & Payload Defense: Adoption of hardware kill switches and decentralized E2EE.
  • Telecom Safeguards: Strict barriers implemented by ISPs against SIM-swaps and SS7 vulnerabilities.
4. Legal & Policy Environment
  • Capacity Building: Aggressively staffing the Data Protection Office to handle abuses.
  • International Partnerships: Alignment with international bodies to combat mercenary supply chains.
  • First-Contact IT Forensics: Deploying well-equipped IT forensic teams to local police stations.
  • Judicial Warrants: Amending RICA 2010 to mandate strict judicial oversight and severe DPPA penalties.
5. Civil Society & Public Awareness
  • Grassroots Threat Network: Campaigns partnering with religious/market groups to push awareness.
  • National Early-Warning: Real-time threat intelligence sharing network among NGOs.
  • Independent Forensics Lab: A bilingual rapid-response clinic to extract payloads and verify infections.
  • Attribution Campaigns: Shifting civil society focus to actively "naming and shaming" spyware operators.

About This Artefact

A supplementary research artefact for the dissertation: "A Framework for Mitigating Spyware-Enabled Surveillance Risks in Uganda" — Ainedembe Denis, Uganda Martyrs University, Masters in Information Systems, 2026.

Data Triangulation

The artefact operationalises Chapter 4 findings by triangulating four independent data sources:

  • SurveillanceWatch.io API — Real-time commercial spyware vendor database with verified country-targeting data.
  • MITRE ATT&CK® STIX — Official behavioural threat intelligence (TTPs) for spyware and stalkerware techniques.
  • Wikipedia REST API — Established historical index of documented spyware programs.
  • Primary Survey Data (N=320) — Quantitative findings on Ugandan user perceptions, behavioral adaptations, and surveillance experiences.

The SFMSEDSR-Uganda Framework

Grounded in Socio-Technical Systems Theory (Trist & Bamforth, 1951), Contextual Integrity (Nissenbaum, 2004), and Privacy by Design (Cavoukian, 2009), the validated framework below was assessed by 8 domain experts (ICT security, legal, civil society, academia) using a modified Delphi method. It achieved an overall expert validation score of 4.41 / 5.0.
